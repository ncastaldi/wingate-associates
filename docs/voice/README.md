# Writing in the owner's voice

All public copy on the site (pages, metadata descriptions, form messages, emails sent to visitors) must sound like the owner, Nathan Castaldi. The source of truth is `voice-profile.json` in this folder, a voice profile generated by Jobsquatch from an interview with him. Read it before writing or editing copy. This page turns it into working rules.

## Rules

- **First person singular.** Write "I", not "we" or "our team". The practice is one person, and the name "Wingate Associates" is allowed to imply more, but the copy never claims a team.
- **Short declarative sentences.** Aim for about 15 words on average and vary the length. Put the point first and the qualification after. Chain clauses with "and" or "but" rather than nesting subordinate clauses.
- **Lean paragraphs.** No more than three sentences, then stop.
- **No em dashes.** Use few commas and rare parentheses. Prefer a full stop.
- **Plain words.** Pick the short Anglo-Saxon word over the Latinate one. Name tools and vendors specifically (Entra ID, Workday, BMC Helix), not categories ("identity platforms").
- **Confidence through specifics.** Let numbers and concrete detail carry the claim (24 roles down to 7, Mean Time to Resolve from 5.05 to 4.65 days). Don't assert greatness. Hedge honestly ("I think") where a claim isn't certain.
- **Write for a smart reader outside IT.** Spell out acronyms and explain jargon the first time they appear on a page. Metric names keep their capitals with lowercase short words ("Mean Time to Resolve"). Plain terms stay lowercase ("managed service provider (MSP)"). Product names (Active Directory, Entra ID) stay as they are, but say what they do the first time.
- **Tone.** Candid, matter-of-fact, a little dry about organisational dysfunction. Never cynical, self-pitying or performatively upbeat.

## Never

- Rhetorical questions ("Sound familiar?").
- A dramatic build to a conclusion, or setbacks reframed as growth.
- Motivational framing, consensus-softening phrases or invented-sounding language.
- These words and phrases: horizontality, synergy, alignment, leverage, circle back, move the needle, at the end of the day, deep dive, bandwidth, touch base, delve, testament, passionate about, thrilled to apply.

## Checking a draft

Before proposing copy, check it against the Never list and count the em dashes (there should be none). Read each paragraph aloud: if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it the way he would say it across a desk.
